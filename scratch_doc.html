1. Why Two Tracks

The original plan sent the same broadcast series to everyone starting the day the landing page went live. But the social plan — the main driver of waitlist growth — does not start until August 11. Beginning a dated series in July means near-silence for early signups before the list has real size; beginning it in late August leaves too few emails for the Closet-Ready thread to work.

The fix is to split the job. A single automated Welcome carries the founder’s why to every subscriber the moment they join, so nobody waits weeks for a first email. Then a tighter scheduled series starts August 11, once social is live, running every ten days through launch.

3. Format Guide — The Recurring Template

Six modules, same order, for both tracks. The only structural difference is the masthead: the Welcome shows a gold band with no date or countdown; every broadcast shows the charcoal band with issue number, date, and days to launch.

Module

What it does

1. Masthead

Broadcasts: charcoal band, e.g. “BROADCAST 2 · AUGUST 21 · 39 DAYS TO LAUNCH.” Welcome: gold band reading “AUTOMATED WELCOME · SENT INSTANTLY ON SIGNUP.”

2. From Anne

60–120 words, first person, one specific angle per issue rather than repeating the same pitch.

3. Feature Sneak Peek

One feature, teased rather than instructed — this audience cannot use the app yet.

4. Style Notes

Short editorial trend note tied to the colour-season framework and the real calendar.

5. The Playlist

A themed, linked Spotify playlist matched to the mood of the issue.

6. Get Closet-Ready

The through-line. Six steps: look, sort, hero pieces, name gaps, group by season, photograph and recap. Step 1 in the Welcome; steps 2–6 across the five broadcasts.

Consistency anchor: Wherever the colour analysis is described, use the locked phrase: “patent-pending colour analysis technology.” Word for word, every time — the same phrase Kyia uses in press and Heather uses in App Store copy. Repetition is what makes it stick.

The Pieces You Keep Reaching Past

Preview text: You don’t need more clothes. You need to know what’s actually missing.

From Anne

I used to buy things I already owned — a slightly different black cardigan, another version of the same white shirt — because I couldn’t picture what I actually had.
